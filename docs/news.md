# News

How news articles are fetched, filtered, linked to events and placed in a region. Back to the [README](../README.md).

`GET /news` is served from a cache that a background job refreshes (`backend/src/services/news.service.js`); requests never call a news provider themselves.

**Request budget.** Each refresh picks the ~10 most important feed events (high severity first, then newest; earthquakes only from the last 24 hours, although the feed shows 7 days), cleans their location text (`"Flood in Nepal 1104225"` → `Nepal`, `"53 km S of Kushiro, Japan"` → `Kushiro Japan`, in `newsLinking.js`) and folds them into at most **3 combined searches** (one per hazard type, the events' place words OR-ed) plus one general disaster search: **at most 4 requests per refresh**. Articles are then linked to events on our side (place words in the title or URL path, hazard words for the general search), deduplicated by URL and near-identical title, and filtered to the event's time window. That linking powers "Show on map" in the news panel and "Related news" in the event drawer.

**Providers** (`backend/src/services/newsProviders/`). Every provider runs and the panel shows their articles merged and deduplicated; each keeps its own last good share, so one that fails (429, timeout, backoff) keeps serving what it fetched before until that expires. **GDELT** DOC 2.0 (every 15 minutes, one broad request: it answers most requests with 429 and takes 15–18 s when it does answer; articles kept 72 h). **The Guardian** Open Platform when `GUARDIAN_API_KEY` is set (every 30 minutes, ≤ 4 requests, so ≤ 200 of the developer key's 500 requests/day; articles kept 24 h). **GDACS media news** (no key, every 30 minutes, articles kept 72 h): for each feed event that links to a GDACS report (EONET wildfires outside the US and floods), the Europe Media Monitor articles GDACS lists for that event, at most 25 events and 8 articles per event, spaced 0.5 s apart; these arrive linked to their event. `DISABLE_GDACS=1` switches it off together with the GDACS severity lookup. Every card says which provider it came from, and `/health` reports each provider's last attempt, last success and last error.

**Relevance.** Every article from every provider must pass the disaster-news rules (`backend/src/services/newsRelevance.js`): non-news sections and formats (sport, business, technology, opinion, culture, interactives, video, …) and review/listing headlines are dropped, as are sports listings ("vs.", "live stream"), product announcements ("tracker", "launches an app"), songs and staged hazards ("pretend typhoon"), retrospectives ("years after", "anniversary", "… after Hurricane Helene" with no impact word in the headline), historical pieces (a past 4-digit year, "N years ago", "World Series") and sports-team uses of a hazard word (a league or sport word and no impact word); a show or election word (musical, film, festival, election, voters, …) drops the headline too, unless it reports impact, a disruption ("called off") or the hazard acting ("hits", "nears"), so "Concert called off as typhoon nears" stays; idioms and names are removed before matching ("perfect storm", "storm of criticism", "landslide victory", "like an earthquake", "row erupts", "Carolina Hurricanes", …); a clear hazard word in the headline keeps an article, an everyday one ("storm", "flood", "tremor") needs impact or weather context. Guardian searches are also restricted to regular articles matched on the headline and standfirst. GDACS media lists are noisy (on 2026-10-16, 30 of 45 articles were off-topic), so the same rules apply to them. A retrospective or historical piece stays only when its headline reports a new event ("new quake", "yeni deprem", "today") or the article is linked to a current feed event; GDACS media lists do not count as a link, because every article in one arrives attached to its event. The same applies in every language, with look-back phrases such as "yıl dönümü", "deprem sonrası kurulan", "aniversario" and "ans après". The rules and the location check run again on the cached articles whenever the cache is rebuilt (including after a restart), so a rule change applies at once; a Guardian article is then dropped only by an explicit marker, since its standfirst is not cached. Article-event linking looks at the headline and the story slug only.

**Location and regions** (`backend/src/services/newsLocation.js`). Each article gets `location: { countries: ["TR"], regions: ["europe"], source }` or `null`. The country comes, in this order, from the linked feed event (its country, derived from the source place name), the article's Guardian keyword tags (`show-tags=keyword`; only the derived codes are kept), the GDACS event it came with (its affected countries), or a GDELT headline that names exactly one country (see below). A fixed ISO country → region table maps it to Africa, Americas, Asia, Europe, Middle East or Oceania (Türkiye under Europe; Russia by the event's longitude). GDACS media lists are noisy, so a GDACS article keeps its event's (or GDACS report's) country only when its headline or story slug names that country (name, alias, demonym), a place of the event label, or an unambiguous city of it (`backend/src/services/newsCities.js`: Istanbul → Türkiye, Padang → Indonesia; shared names such as Tripoli are left out); a city of another country contradicts it. Otherwise it stays unlocated rather than shown in a wrong country. Nothing is guessed: an article none of these place stays unlocated and is listed only under "All regions"; `?region=` never returns it. The panel's region chips are chosen by hand (no geolocation).

**Headline placement** (`location.source: "headline"`). GDELT sends no place for an article and most of its articles match no feed event, so a GDELT article is placed by its headline as the last resort. The headline has to name one country and only one, in the article's own language ("Japonya", "Japón", "Japon"), by the country's name or an unambiguous city from `newsCities.js`; English headlines also count US states and the aliases in `utils/country.js`. Turkish names also match with their case endings, because GDELT drops the apostrophe ("Japonyada", "Türkiyeye"); vowel harmony decides which endings a name takes, so "cadde" (street) is not Chad. Limits, all on the side of leaving an article unlocated:
- A headline naming two countries is not placed, because it does not say which one it is about. A headline naming one country that is not where the event is ("Canada sends firefighters") is placed there, which is the known error of this rule.
- Demonyms do not count ("Indian Ocean", "Turkish Airlines").
- Names that can mean something else do not count (Georgia, Jordan, Chad, Congo, Granada; everyday words such as Turkish "sabah" and "mali"), nor do seas and gulfs that hold a country's name ("Gulf of Mexico", "South China Sea").

**Guardian licence** (developer key, non-commercial): headlines and links are passed through unchanged, Guardian cards never show an image, the "Powered by The Guardian" logo (linking to theguardian.com) is shown next to Guardian content, and Guardian rows are deleted from the cache 24 hours after they were fetched (`NewsCache.expiresAt`). No Guardian article text or article link is stored in the repository (only the logo links to the Guardian home page); the test fixture keeps the response shape with invented headlines and example.com URLs.

**Backoff.** GDELT allows one request every 5 seconds and answers violations with HTTP 429; The Guardian allows 1 request/second and 500/day. All three clients space their requests and, after any failure, back off exponentially with jitter (`backoff.js`: 30 s, 60 s, … up to 15 min, ± 25 %); calls made inside the window fail at once and send nothing, so a 429 is never retried immediately.

**Persistence.** Every successful refresh is written to the `NewsCache` table (one row per article) and loaded back when the backend starts, so a restart or a Render wake-up serves the previous articles immediately with the real `updatedAt`. A failed refresh keeps the previous articles and marks the response `stale: true`; with nothing cached at all the endpoint answers 503 and the panel shows a retry button. `stale: true` is set only when every provider that still contributes articles failed its last attempt (a rate-limited GDELT next to a fresh Guardian share is not stale). Response shape: `{ items: [{ title, url, domain, publishedAt, image, provider, relatedEvent, location }], count, updatedAt, stale, source, provider }`, filterable with `?type=`, `?region=` and `?event=`. `NewsCache.location` (added with the region filter) must exist before a backend that writes it starts: run `npm run db:push` in `backend` against the direct (non-pooler) database host before deploying.

**Languages.** GDELT is asked for articles in English, Turkish, Spanish, French, German and Portuguese. It is still one request per refresh, now for 150 records; the language filter is part of the query. Each article keeps its language (ISO 639-1) and the country of its publisher (`sourceCountry`, as GDELT names it; not where the event is). The Guardian is English with a UK publisher, and GDACS media articles carry no language.
- **English** articles go through all the rules above.
- **Other languages** pass a stricter rule (`backend/src/services/newsLanguages.js`). The section check still applies, and a headline that names a past year is dropped as a retrospective. After that, the headline must name a strong hazard term of its own language: "deprem", "orman yangını", "terremoto", "séisme", "Erdbeben", "inundaciones" and so on. Weak words such as storm or flood never count there, which keeps figures of speech out at the price of some real reports. Figures of speech in that language are removed first ("siyasi deprem", "Erdrutschsieg"), and a headline with a show, election or sport word must also report impact or a disruption.
- **Matching:** Turkish and German terms match the start of a word, because both languages build words with suffixes and compounds ("depremde", "Waldbrandgefahr"). Spanish, French and Portuguese terms match whole words, plurals listed.
- **Events and places:** articles in other languages are linked to events by the same headline rules. A place name spelled differently in that language ("Japonya") leaves the article unlinked rather than wrongly linked, but it can still be placed in that country (see "Headline placement").

**Filters.** `GET /news` takes `country` (ISO 3166-1 alpha-2, matched against the article's location) and `lang` (ISO 639-1, or `unknown`) besides type, region and event. The answer's `languages` counts the matching articles per language before the language filter. The news panel builds its language picker from it, and each card names the language and the publisher's country when the article is not in English. The headline link carries a `lang` attribute, so screen readers read it in that language. News stays out of the public API: The Guardian's developer terms and GDACS media's unclear status do not allow passing articles on.
